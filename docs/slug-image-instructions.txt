SLUG IMAGE GENERATION INSTRUCTIONS
========================================================================

PURPOSE
-------
Generate a complete, consistent set of artwork for ALL 200 slugs (creatures from a Slugterra-inspired
D&D game). The whole roster is being redone in one unified style, so even slugs that already have
art are included. Every slug needs TWO images:
  1. PROTOFORM image -- the slug's calm, cute, non-combat form (portrait).
  2. VELOCITY image  -- the same slug transformed for battle (landscape).
That is 400 images in total. Each slug's section below gives its element, rarity, a
design brief to use for BOTH images (so the two clearly show the same creature), what
each image should show, and the exact output filenames.

Two kinds of slug appear in the list:
  EXISTING slugs -- reference art for them is in ./References. KEEP THE SAME CREATURE: the
    same body shape, colors, markings and distinguishing features as in that reference art (read
    ./References/<filebase>_Proto.png and <filebase>_Velocity.png), rendered in the unified
    style below. Where a slug's text description below mentions an effect the reference art does
    not show, add it to the new image.
  NEW slugs -- no art exists yet. Use the written DESIGN BRIEF to create the creature.

REFERENCE IMAGES -- READ THESE FIRST
------------------------------------
The reference images are found in ./References. Study them before generating anything and
match them: the rendering style, the proportions, the lighting, the level of detail, the way
the protoform and velocity images of the same slug relate to each other, and how each
element's color and effects are used. Files are named <Name>_Proto.png and
<Name>_Velocity.png (spaces removed from the name). When in doubt, the reference images beat
the written style notes below.

STYLE -- PROTOFORM IMAGE (<Name>_Proto.png)
-----------------------------------------
- A single cute, friendly slug (a small, soft-bodied, mollusc-like creature with a stubby
  body, little arms or fins, and a big expressive face) standing upright and facing the
  viewer, full body visible, centered.
- Glossy, polished 3D-render look with soft studio lighting: smooth skin with subtle
  texture, large round glossy eyes with highlights, a simple friendly mouth.
- Portrait orientation, about 3:4 (for example 1024 x 1365 px). Leave a little space
  around the creature.
- Plain very dark background (near black / deep charcoal) with a few ambient accents of the
  slug's element around it and at its feet (for a water slug: droplets and ripples around
  the base). Subtle only -- the creature is the focus.

STYLE -- VELOCITY IMAGE (<Name>_Velocity.png)
-------------------------------------------
- The SAME creature (same colors, markings and distinguishing features as its protoform
  image) transformed into its fierce battle form: sharper, more angular, more muscular or
  armored, aggressive expression, mid-action in a dynamic pose with a strong sense of motion.
- Painterly digital comic / fantasy illustration look: bold clean outlines, saturated colors,
  dramatic lighting and high contrast.
- Landscape orientation, about 3:2 (for example 1536 x 1024 px). The creature is large in the
  frame, with its whole body or at least its head and main weapon-like features visible.
- Dark background filled with swirling energy, particles, debris or splashes of the slug's
  element, flowing around the creature to emphasize the motion. Effects should match what
  the slug actually does (see each slug's velocity description).

TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS (BOTH IMAGES)
-------------------------------------
- PNG files. No text, letters, numbers, labels, logos, signatures or watermarks anywhere.
- Family-friendly, cartoon-style only: no blood, gore, realistic weapons or graphic injury. Show harm with stylised
  energy effects instead. (Some slug descriptions are about bleeding or surgery -- draw the goofy cartoon version.)
- Exactly one creature per image, no other characters, no people, no UI.
- The whole creature must be inside the frame and not cropped awkwardly.
- Consistent look across all slugs: the same rendering style, the same eye style, the same
  level of polish. Slugs should read as one family of creatures.
- The two images of a slug must be obviously the same creature.
- The game shrinks images to 800 px on the long edge, so very high resolutions are unnecessary.

ELEMENT COLOR / EFFECT GUIDE (each slug below lists its element; use the matching palette and effects for the ambient accents and velocity effects)
----------------------------
  Air         pale sky blue, white and silver; swirling wind streaks, feathers, drifting cloud wisps
  Dark        deep violet, black and indigo with a faint purple glow; creeping shadow, smoke and pale moonlight specks
  Earth       browns, ochre and stone grey; rocks, crystals, dust and cracked ground
  Electricity bright yellow and white-blue; crackling arcs, sparks and glowing coils
  Energy      neon cyan and magenta with white-hot cores; pulsing rings, sparks and star-like flares
  Fire        orange, red and gold; embers, flames and glowing cracks
  Healing     soft green, gold and white with warm pink accents; gentle glowing light, leaves, drifting motes
  Ice         icy white, pale blue and cyan; frost crystals, snow, icicles and glints
  Light       white, gold and warm yellow; radiant beams, lens-flare rays and sparkles
  Metal       steel grey, bronze, copper and orange-gold; polished plates, rivets, sparks and metal shards
  None        dull beige and grey; plain and unglamorous, no magical effects at all
  Plant       leaf and moss greens with flower accents; vines, leaves, petals and spores
  Psychic     pink, violet and lilac; soft concentric ripples, floating orbs, dreamy swirls
  Toxic       sickly green and purple; bubbling ooze, drifting spores and noxious haze
  Unique      no single element; choose colors that suit the creature's own design
  Water       blue and teal; water droplets, ripples, splashes and swirling currents

RARITY GUIDE (each slug below lists its rarity tier; apply the matching treatment)
------------
Rarity runs 1 (common) to 10 (legendary). Use it to set how grand each slug looks:
  Common    keep it small, simple and cute; modest, low-key effects.
  Uncommon  a little more detail and personality; clearly visible but still modest effects.
  Rare      more elaborate design and noticeably stronger effects.
  Epic      impressive and detailed; bold, dramatic effects.
  Legendary grand, majestic and highly detailed; spectacular, screen-filling effects.

OUTPUT
------
Save each finished image with the exact filename shown in its section, into a folder of
your choice, and hand the whole set back. In the game's project, they are placed in
docs/Images/, replacing any older files of the same name. Keep names exactly as given,
including capitalization. (Two older files are spelled unusually -- Freightgeist and Hypnogriff --
and the game expects those exact spellings; they are already given correctly below.)

SUGGESTION IF YOUR TOOL HAS A REQUEST LIMIT
-------------------------------------------
It is fine to generate a slug's two images in one request (side by side) or several slugs in
one contact-sheet image (for example a 2x2 or 3x2 grid), then crop them apart afterwards --
as long as every cropped image still meets the requirements above and the creature in each
tile is complete. Four creatures per sheet is a good maximum for quality.
